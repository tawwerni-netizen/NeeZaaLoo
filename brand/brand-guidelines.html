The name

Nizalo means duel.

From the Arabic نزال (nizāl) — a duel, a bout, a contest between two. It is the word an Arabic speaker uses for a championship face-off. The Latinate -o turns a real Arabic noun into a global brand.

It names the format, not the game — which is the whole point. Chess is the flagship, not the category, and the name has to still make sense on the day the game is Speed Math.

The mark

A letterform and a diagram at once.

The mark is the letter N, built from three parts: two vertical bars of identical weight — the two players, and the platform's promise that both receive an equivalent challenge — and one diagonal in Duel Orange, the contest between them.

The N is drawn as a clipped two-colour construction, never as three abutting shapes — abutting paths leave a hairline seam at most raster sizes. That seam is the first thing to check in any rebuild.

Lockups

One accent per lockup.

If the mark carries the orange, the wordmark is monotone. If the wordmark carries it, no mark is present. Two accents in one lockup read as two brands.

In the horizontal lockup the mark sits in a rounded container. This is not decoration: uncontained, the mark reads as a repeated letter and the lockup says "N NIZALO". The container makes it unambiguously an icon.

Never

  • Gradient, glow, bevel, drop shadow, or outline on the mark.
  • Rotate, skew, stretch, or condense any part of the logo.
  • Rebuild the wordmark in a system font — the letterforms are custom paths.
  • Letter-space the Arabic wordmark; tracking breaks connected script.
Colour

One accent, spent carefully.

Duel Orange was chosen against the two obvious alternatives: gold reads casino, and purple-cyan reads crypto. Orange is warm, fast, and competitive, and carries neither association.

The neutrals run deliberately cool against that warm accent. The tension is the brand: a calm, technical ground with one hot signal on top.

Win and loss colour applies to game outcomes only — never to money. A balance is not green when it rises.

Money is rendered in neutral text with tabular figures, because financial UI must read as calm rather than as a scoreboard. Duel Orange means one thing: this is what matters right now — your turn, your clock, the primary action. If a screen has five orange elements, four of them are wrong.

Typography

One superfamily, two scripts.

Archivo supplies display presence and matches the wordmark's geometry. The three IBM Plex faces — Sans, Mono, and Sans Arabic — are one superfamily, so Latin, Arabic, and tabular data speak in a single voice. That coherence matters most in the Arabic UI, where a mismatched pairing is the usual tell that a market was an afterthought.

Every number that represents money, time, or rating is tabular.

A clock whose digits change width jitters; a balance that reflows reads as unstable. Both undermine trust in a product whose central claim is that it counts correctly.

Motion

Motion is confirmation, not decoration.

A move snaps. A result resolves. A clock ticks. That is the entire vocabulary. prefers-reduced-motion is honoured everywhere, and removing motion never removes meaning.

Celebrate the result, never the transaction.

Forbidden outright: slot-machine spins, coin showers, confetti on a deposit, pulsing deposit buttons, and countdown pressure on any financial action.

Voice

Direct. Confident. Never hyped.

Losing is the majority experience in any 1v1 product, and how the brand handles it determines retention more than any win screen. A loss is information, not a wound. Show what changed — rating, what the opponent did better, the rematch button. Never console, never taunt, never upsell.

On money: always show asset and network together — USDT — TRON (TRC20), never just "USDT". State fees before an action, never after. Never imply that winning is likely, typical, or guaranteed.

The rule

Everything descends from one sentence.

The money surfaces should feel like a bank. The game surfaces should feel like a sport. Nothing should ever feel like a casino floor.

When a decision is genuinely close — a colour, a piece of copy, an animation, where the Cash Games tile sits on the homepage — this is the tiebreaker. It has already settled the accent choice, the money-colour rule, the motion ban list, and the homepage hierarchy.
