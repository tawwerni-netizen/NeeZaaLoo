## 1. The strategic constraint the name has to satisfy

Section 2 of the mandate is the whole brief in one line: this is **not**
"chess plus betting", it is **skill, competition, community, progression**.
The name therefore has three hard jobs, in this order:

1. **Survive the game roadmap.** Chess is the flagship, not the category. Any
   name containing *chess*, *rook*, *pawn*, *mate*, *gambit*, *64*, or *check* is
   disqualified on arrival — it would be a permanent tax on Speed Math, Memory,
   Reaction, and every game in Phase 3.
2. **Read as skill, never as wager.** No *bet*, *stake*, *odds*, *jackpot*,
   *win big*, *cash*, *lucky*, *spin*, *pot*. This is why رهان (*rihan*, "a bet")
   was rejected despite being a beautiful Arabic word.
3. **Work natively in Arabic and in English.** Not "translatable" — *native*.
   The strongest outcome is a name that is a real word to an Arabic speaker and a
   clean, confident coinage to an English speaker.

Only one of the three finalists does all three at full strength.

## 2. TOP RECOMMENDATION — **Nizalo**

### Name
**Nizalo** · Arabic wordmark: **نـزالو** · Root: **نزال**

### Meaning
From the Arabic **نزال** (*nizal*) — **a duel, a bout, a contest between two**.
It is the word an Arabic speaker uses for a one-on-one match: a boxing bout, a
championship face-off, single combat. The Latinate `-o` ending turns a real
Arabic noun into a global brand suffix, the same move that gave the world
*Duolingo*, *Vimeo*, and *Klarna*.

Semantically it is **exactly the product**: two people, one contest, decided by
who is better. Not a board. Not a wager. A duel.

### Pronunciation
- **Arabic:** *ni-ZAAL-o* — نِزالو. The root نزال is already in every Arabic
  speaker's active vocabulary; the `-o` reads as a brand ending and causes no
  confusion.
- **English:** *nih-ZAH-loh* (3 syllables, stress on the middle). Contains no
  phoneme that is hard for English, Spanish, French, Turkish, Urdu, Hindi,
  Indonesian, or Portuguese speakers. No spelling ambiguity: heard once, typed
  correctly.

### Brand psychology
The word carries **stakes without money**. A *nizal* is serious — you do not use
it for a casual kickabout — but its seriousness is about **honour and skill**,
not about a pot of cash. That is precisely the emotional register section 3 asks
for: *"I want to test my skill" -> "I can compete" -> "I can climb."*

It also has the right **sound symbolism**: the sharp `z` in the middle gives it
speed and edge, while the open `-alo` ending keeps it warm and invitational
rather than aggressive. It sounds like something you *join*, not something you
*risk*.

For Arabic-speaking users it delivers something no coined English name can: the
platform's core promise is legible in their own language, instantly, without
translation. For everyone else it is a distinctive, premium, unclaimed coinage.

### Taglines
- **English (primary):** *"Your duel. Your proof."*
- **English (alternates):** *"Prove it in one duel."* · *"Two players. One truth."* · *"Think fast. Win anyway."*
- **Arabic (primary):** **نزال واحد يكفي** — *"One duel is enough."*
- **Arabic (alternate):** **أثبت مهارتك** — *"Prove your skill."*

### Potential conflicts
- **Trademark search returned no notable holder.** A web search for "Nizalo" as a
  brand, company, or mark surfaced nothing relevant — only generic trademark-law
  pages and the unrelated mark *NIZIUKO*. This is a strong signal for a coined
  name but is **not clearance**; run the section 4 checklist in
  [NAMING_CANDIDATES.md](NAMING_CANDIDATES.md) before purchase.
- **Nearest phonetic neighbours:** *Nizal* is a surname in some regions, and
  *Nizoral* is an antifungal brand (Johnson & Johnson). Neither is in class 9, 41,
  or 36, and *Nizalo* / *Nizoral* differ in both spelling and stress. Low risk,
  but flag *Nizoral* to counsel explicitly.
- **No negative Arabic meaning** in Gulf, Levantine, or Egyptian dialects. Still
  requires the native-speaker read-aloud check.

### Multi-game scalability: **10 / 10**
"Duel" is the platform primitive, not a game. *Nizalo Chess*, *Nizalo Speed Math*,
*Nizalo Reaction*, *Nizalo Championship* all parse naturally. The name describes
the **format** — 1v1, decided by skill — which is the one thing guaranteed to be
true of every game in Phases 1, 2, and 3.

## 3. RUNNER-UP — **Qimmara**

### Name
**Qimmara** · Arabic wordmark: **قِمّـارا** · Root: **قمة**

### Meaning
From **قمة** (*qimma*) — **summit, peak, the top**. The `-ara` ending softens a
hard consonantal root into a flowing brand name. The promise is **status and
altitude**: the leaderboard, the climb, the top of the mountain.

### Pronunciation
- **Arabic:** *QIM-ma-ra* — قمّارا. The root is instantly recognisable.
- **English:** *kih-MAH-rah*. The initial `Q` without a `U` is exotic and premium
  in English — an asset, not a liability, in the way *Qatar Airways* and *Qonto*
  use it.

### Brand psychology
Where *Nizalo* sells the **match**, *Qimmara* sells the **ranking**. It maps
directly onto section 27's Bronze -> Grandmaster ladder and section 3's *"I can
climb the rankings."* It is the more aspirational, more luxurious of the two, and
it would carry a premium subscription tier beautifully.

Its weakness is the mirror of its strength: it speaks to the **outcome** of
competing rather than the **act** of competing. A new user's first emotion should
be "let me try one", not "let me begin a long ascent". *Qimmara* asks for more
commitment up front.

### Caution
**قمار** (*qimar*) means **"gambling"** in Arabic. *Qimmara* (from قمة, with a
doubled *m*) is a different root, but the two are only one short vowel and one
consonant-length apart in casual speech and are **visually adjacent in Arabic
script**. On a platform that must never read as a casino (section 32), this is a
material risk in exactly the market the name is designed to win. **It requires a
formal Arabic linguistic review before it can be recommended over Nizalo.**

### Taglines
- **English:** *"Climb to the top."* · *"Every rank is earned."*
- **Arabic:** **اصعد إلى القمة** — *"Rise to the summit."*

### Domains

### Potential conflicts
No notable trademark surfaced. Primary conflict is **linguistic, not legal**: the
*qimar* / gambling adjacency described above.

### Multi-game scalability: **9 / 10**
A summit is game-agnostic and pairs naturally with the cross-game Global Skill
Score. Marginally weaker than *Nizalo* only because it describes the ladder
rather than the contest.

## 4. THIRD — **Duelara**

### Name
**Duelara** · Arabic transliteration: **ديـولارا**

### Meaning
*Duel* + the melodic `-ara` suffix. Immediately legible to any English, Spanish,
Italian, Portuguese, or French speaker. Zero explanation required anywhere in the
Western world.

### Pronunciation
- **English:** *doo-eh-LAH-rah*. Effortless.
- **Arabic:** *dyu-LAA-ra* — ديولارا. Pronounceable, but it is a **loanword**, not
  a native word. An Arabic speaker will parse it as a foreign brand name.

### Brand psychology
The safest, most internationally frictionless of the three. It communicates the
1v1 format instantly and is warm and approachable rather than intimidating.

Its limitation is that it is **only** frictionless. It has no native depth in
Arabic, which forfeits the single biggest structural advantage available to this
brand. Note also the **`-ara` / `-lara` ending is a crowded pattern** in consumer
tech naming, which costs distinctiveness.

### Taglines
- **English:** *"Anyone. Anytime. One duel."* · *"Challenge anyone."*
- **Arabic:** **تحدَّ أي شخص** — *"Challenge anyone."*

### Domains
| Domain | Status |
|---|---|
| `duelara.com` | **VERIFIED AVAILABLE** |
| `duelara.app` | **VERIFIED AVAILABLE** |
| `duelara.games` | **VERIFIED AVAILABLE** |
| `duelara.gg` | **UNCERTAIN — REQUIRES REGISTRAR CHECK** |

### Potential conflicts
No notable trademark surfaced. *Duel* is a common descriptive token in gaming, so

narrower protection than the other two.

### Multi-game scalability: **9 / 10**
Same structural advantage as *Nizalo* — it names the format, not the game — minus
the Arabic-native depth.

## 5. Recommendation

**Adopt Nizalo.**

It is the only candidate that is simultaneously:

- a **real word meaning "duel"** in one of the two target languages,
- a **clean premium coinage** in the other,
- **structurally immune** to the chess-to-multi-game transition,
- **free of gambling connotation** in both languages, and
- **verified available on `.com`, `.app`, and `.games`** as of 2026-09-06.

*Qimmara* is the stronger name for status and ranking but carries an unresolved
Arabic risk (*qimar* = gambling) that is disqualifying until a linguist clears it.
*Duelara* is the safe international default and the correct fallback if the
Arabic-market thesis is ever deprioritised.

## 6. Design language direction (applies to whichever name wins)

Per section 36 — premium, intelligent, fast. Deliberately **not** casino.

| Dimension | Direction | Explicitly avoided |
|---|---|---|
| Palette | Deep near-black ground (`#0B0D10`), one confident accent, generous neutral greys | Neon gradients, gold coins, purple-and-cyan crypto palette |
| Accent | A single saturated signal colour used **only** for state that matters: your turn, your clock, your result | Rainbow tiers, five competing accents |
| Type | One grotesque for UI, one tabular-figure face for clocks, ratings, and money | Slab-serif "casino" faces, script logos |
| Arabic type | A properly paired Arabic face (not auto-fallback), full RTL layout | Latin type with Arabic bolted on |
| Motion | Fast, physical, sub-200ms. Motion confirms an action landed | Slot-machine spins, coin showers, confetti on deposits |
| Money UI | Understated, precise, tabular. Money is shown with the calm of a bank | Flashing balances, "YOU WON!" modals, pulsing deposit buttons |
| Imagery | Real players, real focus, real hands | Stock crypto imagery, dice, cards, chips |

The governing rule: **the money surfaces should feel like a bank, and the game
surfaces should feel like a sport.** Nothing in the product should ever feel like
a casino floor.
